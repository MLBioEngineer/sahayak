1. Executive Framing: Screening & Triage vs. Laboratory Diagnosis

In biomedical engineering, diagnostic instruments exist on a spectrum balancing diagnostic granularity against accessibility and cost. Traditional in-hospital Polysomnography (PSG) costs $1,000–$2,500/night and requires 22+ wired sensors, creating an insurmountable barrier that leaves over 80% of sleep apnea patients undiagnosed.

Core Clinical Position: Project Sahayak is explicitly engineered as a pre-hospital clinical screening, early triage, and home monitoring platform—not as an invasive replacement for surgical laboratory PSG. It acts as an affordable first-line filter to detect high-risk patients and route them to hospital specialists.

2. Clinical & Physiological Limitations

2.1 Inability to Differentiate Obstructive (OSA) vs. Central Sleep Apnea (CSA)

Single-lead Lead-II ECG and ECG-Derived Respiration (EDR) detect the physiological consequence of respiratory cessation: thoracic impedance collapse (EDR flatline), vagal bradycardic pauses, and cyclic sympathetic tachycardia. However, it cannot mechanically verify whether the cessation is caused by pharyngeal tissue occlusion (OSA) or a lack of central brainstem neural drive (CSA), which requires supplementary oronasal thermistry or esophageal pressure sensors.

2.2 Absence of Direct Arterial Oxygen Saturation (SpO2)

Clinical AASM scoring standards define hypopnea by concurrent arterial blood oxygen desaturations ($\ge 3\%$ or $\ge 4\%$). Single-lead ECG reconstructs respiratory volume dynamics and autonomic nervous shifts, but does not capture the blood Oxygen Desaturation Index (ODI) directly without an optical pulse oximeter.

2.3 Confounding Co-Morbid Cardiac Arrhythmias

`SleepApneaNet` relies on ventricular depolarization morphology (QRS) and beat-to-beat intervals ($RR_n$). If a patient has pre-existing Atrial Fibrillation (AFib), frequent Premature Ventricular Contractions (PVCs), or severe bundle branch blocks, the underlying electrical chaos can alter baseline rhythm independently of sleep apnea, potentially triggering false positives.

2.4 Inability to Perform Formal Macro-Sleep Staging

Hospital PSG uses multi-channel EEG and EOG to delineate sleep macro-architecture (N1, N2, N3 deep sleep, and REM). Single-lead ECG cannot precisely track sleep stages or determine exact Total Sleep Time (TST), meaning the computed AHI is an estimated nocturnal index based on total recording duration.

3. Hardware, Sensing & Power Constraints

3.1 Motion Artifacts During Restless Sleep

Mechanical friction between the skin, adhesive hydrogel, and lead wires during violent nocturnal turning generates electrochemical half-cell potential shifts: $$V_{\text{total}} = V_{\text{ECG}} + V_{\text{half-cell}} + V_{\text{motion}}$$ Severe gross-body motion can momentarily saturate the 12-bit ADC, corrupting R-peak extraction for 2 to 5 seconds until the AD8232 fast-restore circuit recovers.

3.2 Electrochemical Degradation of Wet Hydrogel Electrodes

Clinical disposable Silver/Silver-Chloride (Ag/AgCl) wet hydrogel electrodes dry out after 8 to 12 hours of exposure to ambient air and body heat, causing increased contact impedance ($Z_{\text{contact}} > 50\text{ k}\Omega$) and potential mild skin irritation over multi-night wear.

3.3 Active Wi-Fi RF Power Consumption & Battery Autonomy

Continuous 100 Hz Wi-Fi batch streaming draws approximately 80–120 mA of current, limiting a compact 500 mAh Li-Po battery to approximately 5.0 to 6.5 hours of continuous operation.

6. Viva Voce & Committee Defense Q&A Script

Q1: Why should a hospital or physician trust a $15 single-lead ECG device over a full hospital sleep study?
"Thank you, Professor. Project Sahayak is explicitly not designed to replace tertiary hospital PSG for complex differential diagnosis or surgical airway planning. Instead, Sahayak is a pre-hospital screening and triage filter. Currently, over 80% of sleep apnea sufferers never receive treatment because an in-hospital test costs $2,500 and has a 6-month queue. Sahayak allows thousands of patients to be screened comfortably at home for $15, routing severe cases to hospitals before they suffer a stroke or heart attack."
Q2: What happens if the patient has Atrial Fibrillation or cardiac arrhythmias? Won't your model produce false positives?
"That is an insightful observation. Pre-existing arrhythmias like AFib or PVCs introduce rhythm irregularities that can mimic autonomic dysregulation. To combat this, Sahayak incorporates a dual-modality safeguard: we don't look at heart rate alone; we simultaneously extract ECG-Derived Respiration (EDR). Even if the cardiac rhythm is irregular, true apnea is physically characterized by a complete flatline of the EDR respiratory envelope, preventing false alarms."
Q3: You don't measure blood oxygen (SpO2). How can you diagnose sleep apnea without knowing if the patient's oxygen dropped?
"You are entirely correct that AASM diagnostic criteria define hypopnea by oxygen desaturation. In Phase 1, our goal was to determine how much diagnostic power could be extracted from a single biopotential lead for sub-$15 cost. EDR captures the mechanical cessation of chest expansion, and Poincaré analysis captures the sympathetic adrenaline surge. Recognizing the clinical importance of blood gas measurement, our Phase-2 design fuses a MAX30102 optical pulse oximeter to synchronously log the Oxygen Desaturation Index (ODI)."
Q4: What happens when the patient rolls over in bed? Doesn't motion artifact ruin your signal?
"Motion artifact is a well-known hurdle. We address this through three layers: first, the AD8232 features an integrated fast-restore circuit that rapidly discharges AC-coupling capacitors following baseline shifts. Second, a zero-phase 4th-order Butterworth bandpass filter strips low-frequency mechanical drift. Third, hardware comparator pins (`LO+ / LO-`) trigger an instant re-connection alert if an electrode physically detaches."
